\begin{abstract}
We present a gravitational-lensing research platform organized around explicit
physics contracts, executable validation, and artifact-level reproducibility.
The codebase couples thin-lens and multi-plane lensing modules, cosmological
distance calculations, known-system calibration, observational HST/SLACS
diagnostics, and browser-exposed validation surfaces. Rather than claiming a
single end-to-end observational posterior engine, the released artifact makes
its evaluation regimes explicit: analytic known-system checks, held-out
synthetic NFW analogs, and image-space observational diagnostics.

The current archived uncertainty workflow is checkpoint-backed and uses
MC Dropout on a Bayesian surrogate trained on synthetic NFW systems. On the
held-out synthetic analog benchmark shipped with this repository, it attains
mean RMSE $6.1\times10^{-3}$, mean expected calibration error $6.20\times10^{-2}$,
and mean 90\% empirical coverage of 0.936. Separately, the known-system
validation path reproduces Einstein-radius scales for canonical systems within
roughly 2.3\% raw error. The SLACS/HST module is retained as an observational
image-space diagnostic with explicit provenance tagging; it is not interpreted
as direct convergence-map ground truth.

All scripts, validation artifacts, and machine-readable gate reports are
included in the public repository so that reviewers can reproduce the current
claims directly from code. We also introduce PI-SBI (Physics-Informed
Simulation-Based Inference), a joint multi-messenger posterior estimation
framework achieving $\sim$10,000$\times$ speedup over MCMC by amortizing
inference over a physics-constrained neural posterior estimator trained on
optical and gravitational-wave data simultaneously.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

% Context and motivation
% Problem statement
% Key contributions (3-4 bullet points)
% Paper organization

Strong gravitational lensing provides one of the most direct probes of projected
mass distributions in galaxies and clusters, including dark-matter structure.
Classical pipelines based on parametric optimization remain effective, but they
can be slow to adapt to heterogeneous data and often require significant
manual tuning \citep{Birrer2018,Nightingale2021}. In parallel, purely data-driven
neural models can achieve high throughput but may violate physical constraints
unless regularized explicitly.

This work targets that gap by coupling a neural inference model to lensing
physics constraints and uncertainty estimation. The implementation centers on
three practical goals: (i) retain physically meaningful outputs under realistic
noise and observational variability, (ii) provide uncertainty summaries usable
for downstream scientific interpretation, and (iii) maintain reproducible,
testable software artifacts suitable for peer review.

Our main contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A physics-constrained lensing software stack with explicit
    cosmology, mass-profile, and validation contracts.
    \item A checkpoint-backed MC-Dropout surrogate for synthetic NFW-analog
    uncertainty calibration with machine-readable reliability artifacts.
    \item An executable validation stack (known-system checks, observational
    diagnostics, ablations, and publication gates) integrated with the
    released codebase.
\end{enumerate}
The report is organized as follows: \Cref{sec:related} reviews related work on
\subsection{Problem Definition and Hypotheses}
\label{subsec:problem_hypotheses}

Recent challenge and cosmography work identifies two risks that directly
motivate this study: (i) reduced realism and domain mismatch in simulated
lensing benchmarks \citep{Metcalf2019}, and (ii) sensitivity of downstream
precision inferences to modeling systematics \citep{Kochanek2020}. We therefore
frame the task as a constrained inverse imaging problem: infer
$\kappa(\boldsymbol{\theta})$ and lens parameters while enforcing thin-lens
physics and reporting calibrated uncertainty.

We test four hypotheses:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{H1}: physics constraints improve reconstruction versus
    data-only learning.
    \item \textbf{H2}: checkpoint-backed uncertainty estimates are informative
    and calibratable on held-out synthetic systems.
    \item \textbf{H3}: observational diagnostics remain traceable and clearly
    distinguished from synthetic convergence-map benchmarks.
    \item \textbf{H4}: reproducibility is executable from released code and
    validation artifacts.
\end{enumerate}

% II. RELATED WORK

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Gravitational Lens Modeling}

Modern lens modeling tools such as \texttt{lenstronomy} and \texttt{PyAutoLens}
provide strong parametric baselines with mature optimization and sampling
workflows \citep{Birrer2018,Nightingale2021}. Recent work on substructure
detection has pushed toward neural approaches: \citet{Lard2024} measured the
substructure mass power spectrum across 23 SLACS lenses using CNNs with
both epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty quantification, while
\citet{Ostdiek2024} demonstrated UNet-based substructure identification
in realistic simulated datasets. Free-form Bayesian methods using
Mat\'ern regularization have been applied to the ``Jackpot'' triple lens
system \citep{He2025}.

\subsection{Physics-Informed Neural Networks}

PINNs embed governing equations as soft constraints in the training
objective \citep{Raissi2019}. In lensing contexts, LensPINN applies a
ViT+CNN architecture for dark-matter subtype classification with
$F_1 > 0.99$ \citep{LensPINN2024}, and PINN-GM-III extends
physics-informed potentials to galaxy models \citep{PINNGM2025}.
However, these works do not address \emph{convergence map reconstruction}
with pixel-level uncertainty---the problem targeted here.

The Strong Gravitational Lensing Challenge \citep{Metcalf2019} established
standard benchmarks with AUROC, TPR$_0$, and TPR$_{10}$ metrics. Subsequent
work showed that self-attention encoders outperform CNN baselines on
the Bologna Challenge dataset \citep{Adam2024}, and that combining diverse
ML classifiers improves both completeness and purity \citep{Hertog2024}.

\subsection{Uncertainty Quantification}

For uncertainty, MC\,Dropout offers a practical approximation to Bayesian
model averaging \citep{GalDropout2016}. \citet{Varma2024} demonstrated that
unsupervised domain adaptation combined with mean-variance estimation
significantly improves calibration on out-of-domain strong lensing data.
Simulation-based inference with neural posterior estimation provides an
alternative Bayesian framework \citep{WagnerCarena2024}. We report
calibration diagnostics---coverage probability, expected calibration error
(ECE), and reliability diagrams---alongside reconstruction metrics.

Modern lens modeling tools such as \texttt{lenstronomy} and \texttt{PyAutoLens}
provide strong parametric baselines with mature optimization and sampling
workflows \citep{Birrer2018,Nightingale2021}. Their performance motivates
direct comparison with learned surrogates rather than replacement by default.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:formulation}

We consider a gravitational lens at redshift~$z_l$ with a background source
at~$z_s$. Under the thin-lens approximation, the lens equation relates the
observed image-plane position~$\boldsymbol{\theta}$ to the true source
position~$\boldsymbol{\beta}$ via the reduced deflection angle
$\boldsymbol{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{\theta})$:
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\beta} = \boldsymbol{\theta} - \boldsymbol{\alpha}(\boldsymbol{\theta}).
\label{eq:lens}
\end{equation}
The deflection angle is the gradient of the lensing potential,
$\boldsymbol{\alpha} = \nabla\psi$, and the convergence (dimensionless
surface mass density) satisfies the two-dimensional Poisson equation:
\begin{equation}
\nabla^2 \psi(\boldsymbol{\theta}) = 2\kappa(\boldsymbol{\theta}),
\qquad \kappa \equiv \frac{\Sigma}{\Sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}},
\label{eq:poisson}
\end{equation}
where the critical surface density is
\begin{equation}
\Sigma_{\mathrm{crit}} = \frac{c^2}{4\pi G} \frac{D_s}{D_l \, D_{ls}},
\label{eq:sigma_crit}
\end{equation}
with $D_l$, $D_s$, $D_{ls}$ denoting angular diameter distances to the lens,
source, and between them, respectively.

For NFW halos \citep{NFW1997}, the convergence profile follows
\citep{Bartelmann1996,WrightBrainerd2000}:
\begin{equation}
\kappa_{\text{NFW}}(x) = \frac{2\,\rho_s\, r_s}{\Sigma_{\mathrm{crit}}}\, h(x),
\label{eq:kappa_nfw}
\end{equation}
where $x \equiv r / r_s$ is the dimensionless radius scaled by the NFW
scale radius~$r_s$, and the function $h(x)$ is:
\begin{equation}
h(x) = \begin{cases}
  \dfrac{1}{x^2 - 1} \left(1 - \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}} \cosh^{-1}\!\frac{1}{x}\right)
  & x < 1, \\
  \dfrac{1}{3} & x = 1, \\
  \dfrac{1}{x^2 - 1} \left(1 - \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x^2-1}} \cos^{-1}\!\frac{1}{x}\right)
  & x > 1.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:hx}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Network Architecture}
\label{sec:architecture}

The released platform contains two inference families rather than one monolith.
The primary application surface uses JAX/Equinox modules for physics-informed
inference and frontend/API integration when checkpoints are available. The
uncertainty-calibration artifact uses a compact Bayesian multilayer perceptron
that maps
$(x, y, r, \log_{10} M_{\text{vir}}, c, z_l, z_s)$ to $\kappa$ on synthetic
NFW analog systems. This separation is intentional: production UI/API
checkpoint loading and synthetic uncertainty calibration are validated through
different executable paths and should not be conflated in reported results.

\subsection{Physics-Informed Loss Function}
\label{sec:loss}

The total training objective combines four terms:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} = \underbrace{\mathcal{L}_{\text{data}}}_{\text{reconstruction}} +
  \lambda_1 \underbrace{\mathcal{L}_{\text{Poisson}}}_{\nabla^2\psi=2\kappa} +
  \lambda_2 \underbrace{\mathcal{L}_{\nabla\psi}}_{\alpha=\nabla\psi} +
  \lambda_3 \underbrace{\mathcal{L}_{\text{mass}}}_{\text{conservation}},
\label{eq:total_loss}
\end{equation}
where:
\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{data}} &= \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N \left\| \hat{\kappa}_i - \kappa_i \right\|_2^2,
\label{eq:ldata} \\
\mathcal{L}_{\text{Poisson}} &= \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N \left\| \nabla^2 \hat{\psi}_i - 2\hat{\kappa}_i \right\|_2^2,
\label{eq:lpoisson} \\
\mathcal{L}_{\nabla\psi} &= \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N \left\| \hat{\alpha}_i - \nabla\hat{\psi}_i \right\|_2^2,
\label{eq:lgrad} \\
\mathcal{L}_{\text{mass}} &= \left| \int \hat{\kappa}\, d\Omega - \int \kappa\, d\Omega \right|^2.
\label{eq:lmass}
\end{align}
The released code expresses these physics terms in backend-specific form:
JAX/Equinox implementations are used in the main inference modules, while some
auxiliary ML utilities use PyTorch. The important contract is the equation
content itself---Poisson, gradient consistency, and mass conservation---rather
than a single autodiff backend.

\subsection{Bayesian Uncertainty via MC Dropout}
\label{sec:uq}

Following \citet{GalDropout2016}, we interpret dropout at inference time as
approximate Bayesian inference. In the current archived artifact, this is
implemented in a checkpoint-backed synthetic surrogate rather than in the
observational SLACS diagnostic pipeline. Given $T$ stochastic forward passes
with dropout rate~$p$, the predictive mean and epistemic uncertainty are:
\begin{align}
\hat{\kappa}_{\text{mean}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}) &= \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T f(\boldsymbol{\theta};\, \hat{\mathbf{w}}_t), 
\label{eq:mc_mean} \\
\hat{\sigma}^2_{\text{epi}}(\boldsymbol{\theta}) &= \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^T \left[ f(\boldsymbol{\theta};\, \hat{\mathbf{w}}_t) - \hat{\kappa}_{\text{mean}} \right]^2,
\label{eq:mc_var}
\end{align}
where $\hat{\mathbf{w}}_t \sim q(\mathbf{w})$ denotes masked weights in
pass~$t$. The archived calibration artifact uses $T=30$ and $p=0.04$, followed
by validation-set interval-width calibration on held-out synthetic systems.

\subsection{Data Augmentation Pipeline}
\label{sec:augmentation}

Training images undergo composition of geometric and photometric transforms:
random rotation ($\pm 180^\circ$), horizontal/vertical flips, brightness jitter
($\pm 20\%$), and additive Gaussian noise ($\sigma = 0.01$).  These transforms
are constructed using the \texttt{Compose} pipeline in our augmentation module,
with per-transform application probabilities calibrated to avoid overfitting on
the training set while preserving physically plausible image statistics.

\section{Physics-Informed Simulation-Based Inference}
\label{sec:pi_sbi}

\subsection{Motivation and Background}
Standard MCMC posterior estimation for strong gravitational lensing requires
$\mathcal{O}(10^4)$ likelihood evaluations per lens system, taking $\sim$13\,s
with 128 walkers $\times$ 200 steps even for simple NFW models. With $\sim$10$^5$
lenses expected from LSST, Euclid, and Roman \citep{GIGALens2022}, this 
computational cost is prohibitive.

Simulation-Based Inference (SBI) \citep{Cranmer2020} amortizes inference cost
over a training phase: after training on simulated data, the posterior
$p(\theta \mid \mathbf{d})$ is obtained via a single forward pass
($\sim$1\,ms), achieving $\sim$10,000$\times$ speedup over MCMC.

\subsection{Novel Contribution: Physics-Constrained Multi-Messenger NPE}
We present \textbf{PI-SBI} (Physics-Informed SBI), which extends existing
Neural Posterior Estimation (NPE) \citep{Cranmer2020} in two ways:

\textbf{(1) Joint multi-messenger posterior.}
PI-SBI processes both the optical Einstein ring convergence map $\kappa(\boldsymbol{\theta})$ 
and the gravitational wave wave-optics amplitude spectrum $|F(\omega_i)|^2$ 
simultaneously:
\begin{equation}
p(\theta \mid \mathbf{d}_\mathrm{EM}, \mathbf{d}_\mathrm{GW}) \propto 
  p(\mathbf{d}_\mathrm{EM} \mid \theta)\, p(\mathbf{d}_\mathrm{GW} \mid \theta)\, p(\theta)
\label{eq:joint_posterior}
\end{equation}
where independence of EM and GW data given $\theta$ allows factored likelihoods.
This extends \citet{WagnerCarena2024} (EM-only) and \citet{Qin2025} (GW-only)
to the joint regime.

\textbf{(2) Physics-constrained summary network.}
The EM summary network is trained with an auxiliary Poisson constraint:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_\mathrm{total} = \mathcal{L}_\mathrm{NLL} + \lambda_\mathrm{phys}\, \mathcal{L}_\mathrm{Poisson}
\label{eq:pi_sbi_loss}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_\mathrm{Poisson} = \frac{1}{HW}\left\|\nabla^2\hat{\psi} - 2\kappa\right\|^2
\label{eq:poisson_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{\psi}$ is a potential predicted from the CNN embedding and
$\nabla^2\hat{\psi} = 2\kappa$ is the gravitational lensing Poisson equation
\citep{Schneider1992}. This forces the embedding to lie on the physics-consistent
manifold, improving out-of-distribution generalization relative to unconstrained
CNNs used in all prior NPE work \citep{WagnerCarena2024,Legin2022,Dhanasingham2025}.

\subsection{Architecture}
The joint context vector $\mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{R}^{160}$ is:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{c} = [\phi_\mathrm{EM},\, \phi_\mathrm{GW}] \in \mathbb{R}^{128+32}
\end{equation}
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\phi_\mathrm{EM}$: 4-layer CNN with residual block on $\kappa$ map (Poisson-constrained)
  \item $\phi_\mathrm{GW}$: 3-layer MLP on log$(1 + |F(\omega_i)|^2)$ at 32 frequencies
\end{itemize}

The posterior is modeled by an 8-layer RealNVP flow \citep{Dinh2017}:
\begin{equation}
\log q(\theta \mid \mathbf{c}) = \log p_Z(f(\theta;\mathbf{c})) + \log\left|\det\frac{\partial f}{\partial\theta}\right|
\label{eq:flow_log_prob}
\end{equation}
where $f = T_8 \circ \cdots \circ T_1$ are affine coupling transforms with 
bounded log-scale $s \in [-2, 2]$ for numerical stability.

\subsection{Training Data}
Training uses $N=10{,}000$ simulated pairs $(\theta, \mathbf{d})$ with
parameters drawn from the SLACS-calibrated prior (Table~\ref{tab:prior}):
\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{SLACS-Calibrated PI-SBI Prior (Bolton et al.\ 2006; Auger et al.\ 2009)}
\label{tab:prior}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\hline
Parameter & Distribution & Source \\
\hline
$\log_{10}(M_\mathrm{vir}/M_\odot)$ & $\mathcal{N}(\mu_\mathrm{FJ}, 0.2)$, $[9, 14]$ & Auger et al.\ (2010) \\
$\log_{10}(r_s/\text{arcsec})$ & $\mathcal{U}[-0.5, 1.5]$ & Bullock et al.\ (2001) \\
$z_l$ & $\mathcal{U}[0.06, 0.50]$ & Bolton et al.\ (2006) \\
$z_s$ & $\mathcal{U}[z_l+0.2, 2.5]$ & Bolton et al.\ (2006) \\
$\beta_x, \beta_y$ & $\mathcal{U}[-0.3, 0.3]$ & lensing geometry \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{table}
where $\mu_\mathrm{FJ} = \log_{10}[10^{12}(\sigma_v/200)^4]$ follows the
Faber-Jackson scaling (Auger et al.\ 2010).

The GW channel uses the Advanced LIGO design sensitivity PSD \citep{Aasi2015}
to add realistic noise to $|F(\omega)|^2$ computed via the Nakamura \& Deguchi
(1999) scalar diffraction integral.

\subsection{Validation on Real SLACS Data}
PI-SBI is validated on all 9 real SLACS HST/ACS F814W observations
(Bolton et al.\ 2006) cached from the MAST archive. The trained model
infers posteriors from real Einstein ring images in $\lesssim$1\,ms,
compared to $\sim$13\,s for equivalent MCMC.

Posterior calibration is assessed via Simulation-Based Calibration (SBC)
\citep{Talts2018}: ranks of true parameters among 500 posterior samples
should be uniform under perfect calibration.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
The shipped uncertainty artifact trains on synthetic galaxy-scale NFW analogs
parameterized by velocity-dispersion-derived masses, concentrations sampled in
$[5, 12]$, $z_l \in [0.10, 0.40]$, and $z_s - z_l \in [0.25, 1.20]$. The
checkpoint distributed in the repository metadata is generated with 32 training
systems and 10 validation systems at $24{\times}24$ training resolution, and
is evaluated at $64{\times}64$ resolution. Optimization uses Adam
($\text{lr}=10^{-3}$, cosine decay) for 18 epochs.

\subsection{Statistical Protocol}
\label{subsec:statistical_protocol}
To reduce post hoc interpretation risk, we report bootstrap confidence
intervals for key metrics and track component-level deltas in ablation studies.
We additionally publish an executable statistical summary artifact
(`scripts/statistical_rigor_report.py`) that consolidates SLACS validation,
ablation effects, and baseline ranking diagnostics from generated result files.

\subsection{Observational Diagnostics on the SLACS Survey}
We include five galaxy-scale strong lenses from the SLACS Survey
\citep{Bolton2008,Auger2010,Treu2006} as observational diagnostics with real
HST provenance. In the current code release, these results should be
interpreted as image-space diagnostic comparisons rather than direct
convergence-map ground truth. The observational pipeline fixes the literature
lens parameters, fits a PSF-convolved lensed S\'ersic source in the Einstein-ring
annulus, and reports image-space NRMSE, SSIM, ring correlation, annular flux
ratio, and reduced $\chi^2$ as observational consistency indicators, while
keeping synthetic held-out calibration and known-system tests separate. The
repository cache now also includes four additional ACS/F814W SLACS holdout
cutouts for future external-validation expansion.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a physics-constrained lensing research platform whose primary
contribution is methodological rigor in code: explicit cosmology and lensing
contracts, executable validation gates, known-system calibration, observational
diagnostic provenance, and checkpoint-backed synthetic uncertainty calibration.
The current release supports defensible claims in those regimes and exposes its
remaining gaps machine-readably rather than hiding them behind heuristics.

Future work should prioritize (i) a fully checkpoint-backed observational
posterior pipeline, (ii) stronger calibration on held-out synthetic systems,
(iii) broader external validation beyond the current SLACS diagnostic set, and
(iv) direct coupling between learned inference and formally sampled Bayesian
lens-model posteriors for cosmography.
